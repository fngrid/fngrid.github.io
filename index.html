What the demo shows

Deploy is the easy part.

The pain starts once the app is live and something breaks at 3 a.m. So the demo spends most of its time after the deploy.

  1. Deploy. One call from your agent to a live URL, and a link for you to keep it.
  2. Operate. Errors group into one issue with the cause, the file, the line and the failing request. Your agent fixes it, replays the request on a zero-traffic preview, then promotes it.
  1. Scale. Spin up a pile of small apps. Each has its own URL, and the bill is one line.
  2. Shut down. Delete what you don't need. Your agent asks for your approval first.
  3. Graduate. Point your own domain at an app, or export one zip with the source, files and a Dockerfile.

First version

  • Node 24 web apps. Express, Hono, Fastify, Next.js standalone.
  • Files and cron. Storage and scheduled jobs included.
  • Logs, env and secrets by API, MCP and CLI. Secret values are never echoed back.

Not at first

  • Python or Go. Node only.
  • A hosted database. Apps bring their own through env vars.
  • Regions. us-east-1 only.
  • Custom domains. Planned right after launch.
Is this live?

No. This page is a concept demo to find out whether the idea is worth building. The video is scripted.

Where would it run?

On AWS, in us-east-1 for now. Apps would run in our accounts, not yours.

Can I leave?

That is the plan. One call exports your code and files with a Dockerfile. Secret values are not exported, and your database stays with your own provider.

Why an agent first?

Coding agents now build working apps in minutes, but getting one onto a real URL still means a person signing up, wiring env vars and reading a dashboard. We want the agent to do that part, with you approving spend and deletes.
